# Reasoning principles by field

## Objective

The question concerns a form of discourse that satisfies **two distinct criteria**:

1. **Epistemic validity**: the discourse is a succession of *correct inferential steps*, that are each reliable, well-justified, resistant to error, and normatively sound.

2. **Cognitive efficacy**: the discourse produces *understanding*, so that the audience grasps _why_ a conclusion holds, with the attendant capacity to generalize, transfer, and act.

> [!QUESTION]
> What principles transform a succession of valid claims into a structured discourse, that yields insight, intelligibility, and transferable understanding?

A succession of valid claims corresponds to a _proof object_ in the logician's sense; understanding is closer to a _proof term with its reduction behavior_ — a structure that reveals _why_ the reduction succeeds.

## Question decomposition

The question divides into a normative, an explanatory and a cognitive research question. The cognitive question bears on the presentation of an argument:

| Question             | Focus                                                                                                                  | Relevant fields (see below)                                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cognitive question   | What makes a reasoning easy to internalize, manipulate, and reuse (explanatory structures that are mentally effective) | - cognitive science <br>- learning sciences<br>- psychology of explanation |

## Main research fields

The most fertile area is the zone where **justification, explanation, and cognition** are studied together.

| Field                                                                                                                                                  | Focus                                                                                      | Concepts                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Functional rhetoric of exposition & Communication theory**                                                                                           | How should reasoning be *presented* to be intelligible for an audience?                    | - salient reasons<br>- memorability                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Learning sciences & Educational psychology**                                                                                                         | How exposition modalities support learning?                                                | - sequencing of concepts<br>- scaffolding<br>- conceptual change<br>- explanatory examples<br>- productive failure<br>- self-explanation<br>- analogical transfer                                                                       |

This table keeps the fields that bear on the presentation of an argument: rhetoric, communication theory and the learning sciences. The fields that bear on epistemic validity, on explanation and on cognition are stated in the [principles by field of `gnomon`](https://github.com/esther-poniatowski/gnomon/blob/ac7816bf9c51630d5024d4c00d1c882a0df833f6/docs/references/reasoning-argumentation-principles-by-field.md).

## Field-by-field principles

### Functional rhetoric of exposition & Communication theory

| Principle                                                                  | Content                                                                                                                                         | Operational consequences                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Audience adaptation** (Perelman & Olbrechts-Tyteca) ^audience-adaptation | A discourse must be adjusted to the conceptual state of its audience, but not by sacrificing rigor.                                             | - tune vocabulary, granularity, and pacing to the audience  <br>- preserve inferential integrity while changing exposition                            |
| **Salience hierarchy** (Perelman) ^salience-hierarchy                      | A discourse must mark hierarchical emphasis: main idea, decisive step, subordinate detail, optional remark.                                     | - visibly distinguish backbone from support  <br>- control emphasis and progression  <br>- prevent peripheral details from masking the core structure |
| **Framing and orientation** (Goffman, Entman) ^framing-orientation         | A discourse becomes more intelligible when the audience knows from the start what problem is being solved and why the proposed route is chosen. | - state the target question  <br>- announce the strategy  <br>- indicate the role of each section in the whole                                        |

### Learning sciences & Educational psychology

| Principle                                                                                                                    | Content                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Operational consequences                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prior knowledge anchoring** (Ausubel) ^prior-knowledge-anchoring                                                           | A discourse becomes understandable when new ideas are *connected to existing conceptual structures*. A higher-order abstraction must be presented before new material.                                                                                                                                                                              | - identify prerequisites explicitly<br>- reactivate the conceptual basis on which the new reasoning will rest<br>- avoid using unprepared notions as silent supports                                           |
| **Progressive disclosure** (Ausubel, Reigeluth) ^progressive-disclosure                                                      | Understanding improves when complexity is introduced gradually: global architecture first, then local elaboration. (*In contrast, many technically valid reasonings present local details before the audience can see their role*.) Elaboration theory prescribes presenting the simplest, most representative overview before elaborating details. | - begin with the target, motivation, and main strategy<br>- then introduce the key ideas<br>- only after that unfold the detailed derivation                                                                   |
| **Conceptual sequencing** (Gagné, Reigeluth) ^conceptual-sequencing                                                          | Understanding depends strongly on the *order in which notions are introduced*.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | - present concepts according to dependency and intelligibility, not only according to formal taxonomy<br>- introduce motivating tensions before abstract resolution<br>- place foundational distinctions early |
| **Scaffolding** (Vygotsky, Wood, Bruner & Ross) ^scaffolding                                                                 | Complex reasoning should be structured through *temporary supports* that make demanding operations manageable.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | - decompose difficult transitions<br>- provide intermediate prompts, schemas, or partial structures<br>- progressively remove support once the pattern is internalized                                         |
| **Worked-example structure** (Sweller, Atkinson, Derry, Renkl & Wortham) ^worked-example-structure                           | A powerful explanation often alternates between general principle and *carefully chosen example*. The example should instantiate the principle, not replace it. Worked examples reduce extraneous load while preserving germane load.                                                                                                              | - use examples to reveal structure<br>- after the example, abstract the transferable pattern<br>- avoid example accumulation without conceptual extraction                                                     |
| **Self-explanation inducement** (Chi, Bassok, Lewis, Reimann & Glaser, Chi & Wylie) ^self-explanation-inducement             | Understanding increases when the audience must *actively reconstruct* why a step is valid or relevant.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | - formulate questions inside the discourse<br>- signal inferential purposes<br>- expose why each step is necessary                                                                                             |
| **Managing misconceptions and naive models** (Posner, Strike, Hewson & Gertzog, Vosniadou, diSessa) ^managing-misconceptions | A discourse should explicitly address and clarify likely *misunderstandings*.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | - identify the most probable false interpretation<br>- contrast the correct structure with the tempting incorrect one<br>- use warnings where ambiguity is systematic                                          |
| **Transfer orientation** (Perkins & Salomon, Bransford & Schwartz) ^transfer-orientation                                     | A strong explanation should support application to *new but structurally related cases*.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | - explicitly state the reusable schema<br>- identify invariant structure across examples<br>- distinguish surface details from deep pattern                                                                    |
| **Feedback-sensitive structure** (Black & Wiliam, Sadler) ^feedback-sensitive-structure                                      | Understanding is strengthened when the discourse includes ways to *diagnose* whether the intended structure has actually been grasped or whether gaps remain.                                                                                                                                                                                       | - include checkpoint questions<br>- formulate local tests of comprehension<br>- distinguish recognition from genuine reconstruction                                                                            |
